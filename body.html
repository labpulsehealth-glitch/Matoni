Your body is not a mystery you should be afraid to understand.

Learn about reproductive anatomy, hormones and the menstrual cycle through accessible explanations designed to build understanding without shame or judgement.

Understanding your body is a lifelong journey.

The female body is more than its reproductive system. This learning experience introduces some important parts of reproductive health. It is an educational starting point, not a complete account of every aspect of women's health.

01 · REPRODUCTIVE ANATOMY

Get to know the reproductive system.

Select an organ to learn about its general location and function. These descriptions are simplified for learning.

SELECTED ORGAN

Ovaries

The ovaries are two small organs located on either side of the uterus. They contain egg cells and produce hormones, including oestrogen and progesterone.

Why it matters

Ovarian activity is involved in the menstrual cycle, hormone production and reproductive function.

Anatomy varies between individuals. Not everyone has the same reproductive organs or anatomy.

02 · HORMONES

Meet the hormones involved in the menstrual cycle.

Hormones are chemical messengers. They help coordinate processes in different parts of the body.

FSH

Follicle-stimulating hormone

Produced by the pituitary gland. It helps stimulate the growth of ovarian follicles, which contain developing egg cells.

LH

Luteinising hormone

Produced by the pituitary gland. A surge in LH usually triggers ovulation.

OESTROGEN

Oestrogen

A group of hormones with several functions. During the menstrual cycle, oestrogen helps the uterine lining grow.

PROGESTERONE

Progesterone

After ovulation, progesterone helps prepare and maintain the uterine lining for a possible pregnancy.

Hormones also influence other body systems. Their effects are not limited to menstruation or reproduction.
03 · THE MENSTRUAL CYCLE

Explore the phases of the menstrual cycle.

Select a phase to learn what commonly happens. Cycle timing differs between individuals, and the phases are not identical in everyone.

PHASE 01

Menstruation

Menstruation is when blood and tissue from the uterine lining leave the body through the vagina. It marks the beginning of a new menstrual cycle.

What happens?

The uterine lining is shed because pregnancy has not occurred in that cycle.

What should you know?

Bleeding patterns and duration vary. Severe pain or unusually heavy bleeding deserves medical attention.

Remember: The often-quoted 28-day cycle is an illustrative average, not a rule. Ovulation does not always happen on day 14. Cycle tracking cannot reliably confirm pregnancy or prevent pregnancy on its own.

04 · BODY KNOWLEDGE

Things worth remembering.

Every cycle is not exactly 28 days.

Cycle length varies. A single average should not be treated as a universal standard.

Menstrual pain should not be dismissed.

Some discomfort can occur, but severe or disruptive pain deserves assessment.

Menstruation is not dirty.

Menstruation is a normal biological process. It is not a reason for shame or exclusion.

Symptoms do not diagnose conditions.

Similar symptoms can have different causes. A healthcare professional can help assess persistent concerns.
