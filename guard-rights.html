Knowledge is a form of protection

Your rights. Your voice. Your dignity.

Every girl and woman deserves to live with dignity, make informed decisions about her life, pursue an education, feel safe in her community and be treated with respect.

You do not have to be powerful, wealthy or influential to deserve your rights.

Something every girl should know

You are a person with rights, not someone else's property, possession or permission to control.

The foundations

Rights every girl and woman should know.

These are important human-rights principles. The exact laws, procedures and available remedies depend on the country and circumstances. Understanding these principles can help you recognise when something is wrong and where to seek guidance.

♡

Right to dignity

You deserve to be treated as a human being with worth. Humiliation, degrading treatment, violence and abuse should never be normalised.

⌂

Right to safety

You deserve protection from violence, threats, exploitation and abuse. Your home, school, workplace and community should not be places where harm is excused.

✿

Right to bodily autonomy

Your body matters. You deserve information, respect and freedom from unwanted sexual contact, coercion and harmful treatment.

✎

Right to education

Girls deserve opportunities to learn, develop their abilities and pursue their ambitions without discrimination, harassment or violence.

☼

Right to health

You deserve access to appropriate healthcare and understandable health information, including respectful care for sexual and reproductive health needs.

◉

Right to privacy

Your personal information, private communications and personal experiences deserve respect. Your story should not become public entertainment.

⚖

Right to equality

You deserve fair treatment and equal dignity. Your gender should not be used as an excuse to deny you opportunities or subject you to discrimination.

♧

Right to seek help

You should be able to seek information, healthcare, protection, legal guidance and support when you experience harm.

✧

Right to participate

Your opinions and experiences matter. Girls and women deserve opportunities to participate in decisions that affect their lives and communities.

Remember: Having a right does not mean that every institution will automatically respect it. Knowing your rights can help you identify mistreatment, ask questions, seek support and understand the protections that may be available.
Consent and personal boundaries

Your body is not a public possession.

Consent is an essential part of respectful relationships and interactions.

♡

Your boundaries deserve to be respected.

What consent means

Consent means freely agreeing to something. It should be given without force, threats, manipulation or pressure. It must relate to the specific activity being discussed, and a person can change their mind.

Silence, fear, freezing, unconsciousness or the absence of physical resistance should not be treated as permission.

Being in a relationship or marriage does not mean someone has unlimited permission to another person's body. Each person deserves respect and freedom from sexual violence and coercion.

Age, capacity and applicable laws also matter. A child's apparent agreement does not make sexual exploitation or abuse acceptable.

Recognising violations

When might someone's rights be violated?

These examples can help you recognise behaviour that should not be dismissed as normal, traditional or deserved.

1

Being forced into marriage

Being threatened, pressured or made to marry without free and full consent. Child marriage also raises serious protection and legal concerns.

2

Being denied education

Being prevented from attending school or pursuing learning opportunities because of gender, harmful discrimination or coercion.

3

Experiencing sexual violence

Unwanted sexual contact, assault, exploitation, harassment or being pressured into sexual activity.

4

Being controlled or threatened

Someone using fear, intimidation, isolation, financial control or threats to restrict your freedom.

5

Being denied healthcare

Being humiliated, discriminated against or denied appropriate care because of your gender or personal circumstances.

6

Being exploited online

Someone sharing intimate images without consent, threatening you, impersonating you or exposing your private information.

When something is wrong

What can you do if your rights are violated?

You may not be able to change the situation immediately. But you can explore ways to protect yourself and find appropriate support.

1

Prioritise your safety

If someone is threatening or hurting you, consider moving to a safer place and contacting a trusted person or appropriate emergency service.

2

Speak to someone trustworthy

Consider a responsible adult, qualified professional, counsellor, healthcare provider or reputable support organisation.

3

Learn about your options

Ask a qualified local legal professional or appropriate support organisation about the laws, protection measures and procedures that may apply.

4

Keep relevant information safely

If it is safe, retain relevant messages, dates, photographs or documents. Do not risk your safety to collect evidence or confront someone.

5

Consider appropriate reporting channels

You may be able to contact relevant protection services, law enforcement, regulators or other authorised institutions. Ask about confidentiality and what happens after a report.

6

Seek continued support

Healthcare, emotional support, safe accommodation, education assistance and legal guidance may all be important parts of protecting your wellbeing.

For girls under 18

You have the right to protection.

Children and teenagers deserve to grow up in environments where their safety, development, education and wellbeing are taken seriously.

Important things to understand

  • Children deserve protection from abuse, exploitation, neglect, trafficking and harmful practices.
  • A child should not be blamed for abuse committed against them.
  • Children should have opportunities to learn, develop and participate in decisions affecting them in ways appropriate to their age and understanding.
  • If a child is in danger, a safe adult or appropriate child-protection professional should be contacted.
  • If a parent or caregiver may be responsible for the harm, another safe adult or appropriate protection service should be approached.
  • Adults and professionals may have legal safeguarding responsibilities. A child should not be promised absolute secrecy where protection requires action.
Understanding the Nigerian context

Know where to look for legal information.

Nigeria has constitutional protections and laws addressing different forms of violence, discrimination and child protection. Their application and available remedies can depend on the issue, location and relevant jurisdiction.

The Constitution of Nigeria

The Constitution provides an important framework for fundamental rights, including protections relating to life, dignity, personal liberty, private and family life, freedom from discrimination and freedom of expression. The precise scope and enforcement of these protections may require legal interpretation.

Child protection laws

The Child Rights Act provides a national framework for children's rights. State-level adoption, implementation and relevant legal procedures matter, so it is important to check the law applicable in your state.

Violence against persons legislation

The Violence Against Persons (Prohibition) Act addresses several forms of violence and harmful practices. Its application outside the Federal Capital Territory depends on relevant state legislation and implementation.

Other protections

Other federal and state laws may address trafficking, sexual offences, workplace protections, education, discrimination and access to justice. The right legal pathway depends on the particular circumstances.

Important: Do not assume that a law applies in exactly the same way in every Nigerian state. For a specific situation, consult a qualified legal practitioner or an appropriate legal-aid organisation in the relevant location.
What MATONI believes

Your rights should not depend on your status.

Not your income. Not your family background. Not your marital status. Not whether you are popular, educated, married, unmarried, employed or unemployed. Not whether someone believes your story immediately. Every girl and woman deserves dignity, safety, respect and access to appropriate support. We believe that knowledge should be accessible, understandable and free from shame.
