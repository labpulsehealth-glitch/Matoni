HERBODY | BODY LITERACY

Understand your menstrual cycle.

Your menstrual cycle is more than your period. Learn what happens in your body throughout the cycle.

What Is the Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is a series of natural changes controlled by hormones. These changes prepare the body for a possible pregnancy. If pregnancy does not occur, the lining of the uterus is shed through menstruation.

A cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the day before the next period begins.

Explore the Four Phases

Select each phase to understand what happens. The timing varies from person to person.

Menstrual Phase

This is when menstrual bleeding occurs. The lining of the uterus is shed and leaves the body through the vagina.

Bleeding commonly lasts around 2–7 days, although individual experiences vary.

Follicular Phase

This phase begins on the first day of menstruation and continues until ovulation. Follicle-stimulating hormone helps follicles in the ovaries develop. Usually one egg becomes dominant.

Oestrogen levels rise and the uterine lining begins to rebuild.

Ovulation

An increase in luteinising hormone triggers the release of an egg from an ovary.

Ovulation does not always happen on day 14. Its timing can vary, even in people with regular cycles.

Luteal Phase

After ovulation, the body produces more progesterone. This hormone helps maintain the uterine lining.

If pregnancy does not occur, hormone levels fall and another menstrual period begins.

Does Everyone Have a 28-Day Cycle?

No. A 28-day cycle is often used as an example, but menstrual cycles naturally vary. In adults, cycles around 21–35 days are common. Cycles can also be less predictable during adolescence.

Do not worry if your cycle does not match an example perfectly. However, significant changes or concerning symptoms deserve professional attention.

Remember

Your cycle is personal. Learning about it can help you recognise patterns and communicate changes to a healthcare professional.
