WHY HERGUARD EXISTS

Safety should never be a privilege.

Violence, harassment, coercion, exploitation and abuse can happen in homes, schools, workplaces, relationships, communities and online spaces.

Sometimes the people who should provide protection are the very people causing harm. Sometimes survivors are ignored, blamed, threatened or discouraged from seeking help.

HERGUARD is MATONI's proposed protection and safeguarding division. Its purpose is to develop safer pathways through which girls and women can access information, support, referrals and appropriate protection services.

We believe that seeking help should never require someone to surrender their dignity, privacy or voice.

UNDERSTANDING VIOLENCE

Sexual violence and harassment

Sexual violence includes acts or attempts involving sexual activity without freely given consent. It can occur in relationships, families, institutions, workplaces and other settings.

Harassment may involve unwanted sexual comments, advances, intimidation, coercion or other inappropriate conduct.

Important things to understand

  • Consent must be freely given.
  • Silence or fear does not mean consent.
  • A person's clothing, relationship status or past experiences do not justify abuse.
  • Survivors are not responsible for the actions of perpetrators.
  • Medical attention may be important even when there are no visible injuries.
  • Survivors should be treated with dignity and without blame.

Anyone experiencing sexual violence may need immediate safety assistance, medical attention, emotional support or access to appropriate protection and legal services.

PROTECTING CHOICE AND CHILDHOOD

Forced and child marriage

Marriage should never be imposed through threats, intimidation, violence, manipulation or coercion.

Child marriage can interrupt education, expose children to serious health risks and restrict their freedom, development and future opportunities.

Warning signs may include:

  • Threats or pressure to marry against one's wishes.
  • Plans to remove a child from school for marriage.
  • Restriction of movement or communication.
  • Threats of violence or punishment for refusing marriage.
  • Travel arrangements made without a child's free agreement.

Anyone facing forced marriage or concerned about a child at risk should seek assistance from appropriate child-protection services and trusted safeguarding professionals.

DIGNITY WITHOUT EXCEPTION

Bullying and body shaming

Nobody deserves to be humiliated because of their appearance, body shape, skin condition, disability, background, health condition or personal circumstances.

Bullying can take place in schools, homes, workplaces, communities and digital spaces. It may involve repeated insults, threats, exclusion, intimidation or public humiliation.

Remember:

  • Your body is not an invitation for humiliation.
  • Disability and health conditions should never be used to ridicule someone.
  • Online harassment can have serious real-world consequences.
  • Seeking help is not weakness.
RECOGNISING HARM

Abuse at home or in relationships

Abuse is not always physical. It can involve controlling behaviour, threats, isolation, humiliation, financial control, intimidation and emotional manipulation.

Possible warning signs include:

  • Someone constantly monitors or controls your movements.
  • You are threatened when you disagree or attempt to leave.
  • Someone controls your money, education or access to other people.
  • You are repeatedly insulted, humiliated or intimidated.
  • You are pressured into sexual activity or other unwanted actions.

If you recognise these experiences, you deserve support. You do not have to prove that your situation is serious enough before seeking help.

SAFETY IN DIGITAL SPACES

Online abuse and exploitation

Digital spaces should not become places where people are threatened, manipulated, exploited or humiliated.

Online abuse may include:

  • Cyberbullying and targeted harassment.
  • Threats to publish private images or information.
  • Blackmail and sexual extortion.
  • Unwanted sexual messages or explicit material.
  • Stalking, impersonation and account harassment.
  • Grooming and exploitation of children.

Where safe to do so, preserve relevant evidence, avoid confronting a threatening person alone, and seek help from trusted professionals and appropriate reporting channels.

Do not share intimate images further in an attempt to prove that abuse occurred. For children, suspected sexual exploitation should be handled through appropriate child-protection channels.

SUPPORT WITHOUT JUDGEMENT

Survivors deserve care, not blame.

People who have experienced abuse may need different forms of assistance. Support should respect their choices, safety, privacy and dignity.

Medical support

Access to qualified healthcare professionals for appropriate assessment, treatment, documentation and follow-up care.

Psychological support

Access to trained counsellors, mental health professionals and appropriate psychosocial care.

Legal guidance

Information about legal rights and access to qualified legal professionals and appropriate protection services.

Protection and safe accommodation

Where necessary, referrals to appropriate protection services and verified safe accommodation providers.

FINDING THE RIGHT HELP

Find trusted support.

A person experiencing abuse may need help from several different services. Depending on the situation, these may include:

  • Hospitals and qualified healthcare providers.
  • Sexual assault referral centres.
  • Child-protection and social welfare services.
  • Qualified legal aid providers.
  • Verified domestic violence and survivor support organisations.
  • Appropriate law enforcement and protection authorities.
  • Qualified mental health and psychosocial professionals.

MATONI intends to develop a directory of verified support services with relevant locations, contact details, service descriptions and referral information.

The support directory is a planned feature. A verified, active referral directory is not currently available through this page.
KNOWLEDGE IS PART OF PROTECTION

Know your rights.

Every person deserves dignity, personal safety and protection from violence, exploitation and discrimination.

Women and girls should have access to understandable information about their rights, available protection systems and the services that may assist them.

Our proposed rights and protection resource will cover:

  • Protection from violence and abuse.
  • Child safeguarding and protection.
  • Consent and bodily autonomy.
  • Access to appropriate healthcare.
  • Privacy and confidentiality.
  • Access to legal information and assistance.
  • Protection from harassment and exploitation.

Laws and protection procedures differ by location. Legal information published by MATONI must be reviewed against current applicable laws and qualified local legal guidance.

OUR FUTURE RESPONSE MODEL

What HERGUARD is working towards.

We do not want protection to end with a list of telephone numbers. Our long-term vision is to build a responsible support system that can help people navigate the process of seeking assistance.

01

Reach out safely

A person can choose a suitable way to contact the service and communicate their needs.

02

Understand the situation

A trained responder assesses urgency, immediate safety concerns and the kind of assistance requested.

03

Connect with appropriate support

The person is guided towards suitable healthcare, protection, legal or psychosocial services.

04

Follow up responsibly

Where consent and safe contact arrangements allow, the service follows up to understand whether the person has accessed support.

This is a proposed response model. HERGUARD does not currently operate a staffed reporting or emergency response service.
YOUR SAFETY COMES FIRST

Think carefully about your safety.

If someone monitors your phone, messages, internet activity or movements, seeking help may require additional safety planning.

  • Use a safer device if you believe yours is monitored.
  • Consider whether your browser history or messages can be seen by another person.
  • Contact someone you trust if doing so is safe.
  • Avoid announcing plans to leave an abusive situation if this could increase danger.
  • Seek guidance from qualified domestic violence or protection professionals when possible.

Leaving an abusive situation can sometimes increase immediate danger. A safety plan should consider the person's circumstances and risks.

This website cannot provide an emergency extraction, transport or rescue service.

YOUR VOICE DESERVES TO BE HEARD

Report abuse.

We envision a secure reporting pathway through which individuals can request support, report safeguarding concerns and communicate their preferred method of contact.

The future system is intended to provide options such as secure online reporting, telephone, WhatsApp, SMS and email, subject to appropriate security and operational arrangements.

Reporting is not yet available.

This page does not currently collect abuse reports or transmit information to a response team. Please do not submit personal details, survivor accounts or sensitive information through this prototype.

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, seek assistance from an appropriate local emergency service or trusted protection professional.

THE MATONI COMMITMENT

We believe every girl deserves protection.

No victim-blaming. No humiliation. No discrimination. No exploitation. No dismissal of someone's suffering.

We believe that protection should be accessible, support should be compassionate and every person should be treated with dignity.
