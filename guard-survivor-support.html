A space for care and healing

You are still whole.

Whatever happened to you, it does not reduce your worth. You deserve kindness without conditions, support without pressure, and a future that is bigger than the pain you have experienced.

You do not have to be okay today. You deserve care today, exactly as you are.

Your story is still being written.

Let this be said clearly

What happened to you is not your identity. It is not your fault. And it does not decide your future.

First, a few things to remember

You deserve support that respects you.

Survivor-centred support begins with the person who experienced the harm. It does not take control away from them or demand that they heal in a particular way.

♡

You deserve to be believed

You should not have to prove your pain to deserve compassion, safety or support.

✿

Your choices matter

You deserve information and support without being forced into decisions you are not ready to make.

☼

Your dignity is yours

You are not dirty, ruined, weak or less worthy because someone hurt you.

⌂

Safety comes first

You deserve to be in an environment where your wellbeing is taken seriously.

✧

Healing has no deadline

There is no correct speed for healing. You do not owe anyone a recovery timetable.

❀

You are more than the experience

Your dreams, personality, talents, relationships and possibilities still belong to you.

❀
“I am allowed to take my time. I am allowed to ask for help. I am allowed to build a life that feels safe and beautiful again.”

You do not have to say these words if you are not ready. Let them simply be here for you.

If this has happened to you

You do not have to figure everything out at once.

There is no perfect script for what to do after experiencing abuse, violence, harassment or exploitation. Start with what is safest and most important right now.

1

Think about your immediate safety

If you are in danger, move towards a safer place if you can. This might be with a trusted person, in a public place or somewhere the person causing harm cannot easily reach you.

2

Reach out to someone safe

You might choose a trusted friend, relative, teacher, counsellor, healthcare professional or another responsible adult. You can share only what you feel comfortable sharing.

3

Consider medical care

If you have been injured, sexually assaulted or exposed to a possible health risk, a healthcare professional can discuss care and time-sensitive options. You do not need to decide about reporting before seeking medical help.

4

Give yourself room to breathe

You do not have to make every decision immediately. If possible, take things one step at a time and let a safe person help with practical needs.

Your reaction does not make your experience less real. People respond to trauma differently. Some cry; others feel numb, confused, angry, frightened or disconnected. Some struggle to remember details. Some continue their everyday routines. There is no single correct way to react, and none of these responses makes what happened your fault.
For the people who care

How to support someone who has been hurt.

Your response can make a difference. You do not need perfect words. You need patience, respect and a willingness to listen.

Words that can make someone feel less alone

I believe you.
I am sorry this happened to you.
This was not your fault.
You do not have to tell me everything.
What would help you feel safer right now?
I will respect your choices.
Would you like me to stay with you?
You deserve support, and we can take this one step at a time.

Things to avoid saying or doing

  • Asking why they did not fight back, leave sooner or report immediately.
  • Blaming their clothing, behaviour, relationship or decisions.
  • Pressuring them to confront the person responsible or forgive them.
  • Sharing their story with friends, family or social media without permission.
  • Making promises you cannot keep, including absolute secrecy.
  • Turning their experience into gossip, an investigation or a story about yourself.
Care beyond the first conversation

Support can look different for everyone.

There is no one-size-fits-all recovery plan. These are some forms of support a survivor may choose to explore.

Medical and physical care

A healthcare professional can assess injuries, discuss sexual and reproductive health needs, explain available preventive care and answer questions. Some options are time-sensitive, so seeking care promptly may be important. You deserve respectful treatment without shame.

Emotional and psychological care

A trained counsellor or mental health professional may help you process difficult feelings, understand your responses and explore coping strategies. You can ask about the person's qualifications, confidentiality and approach before beginning.

Practical and everyday support

Support may include a safe place to stay, help with transport, food, school arrangements, childcare or accompanying someone to an appointment. Small acts of care can matter enormously.

Community and trusted relationships

Some people find comfort in trusted friends, family, peer groups or supportive community organisations. Choose people who respect your privacy and do not pressure you to share more than you want.

Your voice matters

Reporting is a choice where the law allows you to decide.

You may want to report what happened, seek legal advice, explore formal protection or take time before deciding. You deserve clear information about possible processes and their consequences, without threats or pressure. There may be legal safeguarding or reporting duties in situations involving children or immediate danger. A qualified local professional can explain what applies. Accessing healthcare and emotional support should not be treated as something you must earn by making a report.

When the survivor is under 18

Children deserve protection, not blame.

When a child or teenager experiences abuse, exploitation or violence, their safety and protection must be taken seriously.

How a safe adult can help

  • Listen calmly and take what the child says seriously.
  • Explain that they are not to blame and did the right thing by seeking help.
  • Help them reach a safe, responsible adult or qualified child-protection professional.
  • Do not confront or alert a suspected abuser if doing so could increase danger.
  • Do not repeatedly question the child or pressure them to recount painful details.
  • Do not promise absolute secrecy. Explain gently that information may need to be shared with people responsible for keeping them safe.

Where a parent or caregiver may be involved in the harm, seek help from another safe adult or appropriate child-protection professional instead.

Protecting your privacy

Your story deserves careful handling.

If you are seeking help, you may want to think about who can access your information and whether your devices are safe to use.

Choose your confidants Share personal information with people and professionals you have reason to trust.
Protect your devices Review passwords, account access and location sharing if someone may be monitoring you.
Be careful online Avoid posting identifying details or confronting an abuser publicly if it could put you at risk.
Keep evidence safely if you choose Messages, screenshots or other records may be useful. Store them somewhere the person causing harm cannot access.
Ask about confidentiality Before sharing sensitive details with a service, ask who may see the information and when it may need to be disclosed.
Do not delay urgent care Never put your health or immediate safety at risk to collect evidence or preserve a device.

If you are in immediate danger

Please prioritise getting to a safer place and contacting local emergency services, a trusted safe adult or a nearby healthcare provider. If you are injured or need urgent medical attention, seek care as soon as possible. If one person does not take you seriously, try another safe person or appropriate service. You deserve to be helped.

♡

There is still room for joy in your story.

You can laugh again. You can dream again. You can discover new things about yourself, build meaningful friendships and create a future that belongs to you. Healing may take time, and difficult days may still come. None of that takes away your worth.
