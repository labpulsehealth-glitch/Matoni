Online Abuse &
Exploitation

The internet can help us learn, connect, create and express ourselves. But it can also be used to threaten, manipulate, exploit, stalk and humiliate people.

You deserve privacy and safety online just as much as you deserve them offline. Having a social media account, sharing a photograph or trusting someone does not give anyone permission to exploit you.

⌘
Your online presence should never cost you your safety.
SAFETY IN EVERY SPACE
KNOW WHAT TO LOOK OUT FOR

What does online abuse look like?

Online abuse can happen through social media, messaging applications, gaming platforms, email, websites, video calls and other digital spaces. It may be carried out by strangers, acquaintances, partners or people who have access to your personal information.

01

Cyberstalking

Repeated unwanted online contact, monitoring your accounts, tracking your digital activity, sending threatening messages or using technology to follow and intimidate you.

02

Online grooming

Building trust with a child or vulnerable person to manipulate them for sexual exploitation, abuse, private images or other harmful purposes. It can involve gifts, attention, secrecy and emotional pressure.

03

Sextortion

Threatening to share intimate images, videos or private conversations unless someone sends money, more images, sexual content or does something demanded by the offender.

04

Image-based abuse

Sharing, distributing or threatening to distribute intimate images without consent. This may include images obtained through deception, coercion or unauthorised access.

05

Impersonation

Creating fake accounts, pretending to be another person, using someone's photographs without permission or contacting others in their name to damage their reputation.

06

Doxxing

Publishing someone's private information, such as their address, phone number, workplace, school or other identifying details, to expose, intimidate or encourage harassment.

07

Online harassment

Repeated insults, threats, unwanted sexual messages, humiliating comments, coordinated attacks or abusive messages sent through digital platforms.

08

Non-consensual AI-generated images

Using artificial intelligence or editing tools to create fake sexual or humiliating images or videos of someone without their consent.

09

Digital surveillance

Secretly accessing accounts, installing monitoring software, tracking location or using devices and applications to monitor someone without their knowledge or permission.

UNDERSTANDING ONLINE GROOMING

Not everyone who gives you attention has good intentions.

Online grooming is a process in which someone builds a relationship with a child or vulnerable person to manipulate, exploit or abuse them. It can happen gradually, and the person may initially appear caring, generous or trustworthy.

Unusual attention and gifts

Someone gives excessive compliments, gifts, money, airtime or special treatment to create a sense of obligation or trust.

Requests for secrecy

They insist that conversations, gifts, meetings or relationships must be hidden from parents, guardians or other trusted people.

Testing boundaries

They gradually introduce sexual topics, ask personal questions, request private images or test how much the person is willing to keep secret.

Threats and manipulation

They may threaten exposure, withdraw affection, use guilt, claim the person owes them something or threaten to harm themselves or others.

Important: Grooming is not the victim's fault.

A child may trust, like or feel emotionally attached to the person exploiting them. That does not make the exploitation their responsibility. The responsibility belongs to the person who manipulates and abuses their trust.

WHEN SOMEONE THREATENS YOU

Sextortion: threats are a form of exploitation.

Sextortion happens when someone uses intimate images, videos or other private material to threaten and control another person. They may demand money, more images, sexual acts or continued contact.

They threaten to expose you

They say they will send images to your family, friends, classmates, employer or social media followers.

They demand money

They demand payment and may continue making new demands even after receiving money.

They demand more content

They threaten exposure unless you send additional images, videos or engage in sexual activity.

They use fear to keep control

They may create deadlines, pretend to have already shared the content or claim that they know where you live.

If someone is blackmailing you, remember this:

You are not responsible for another person's threats. Paying does not guarantee that they will stop. Do not send more material to satisfy their demands. If possible, preserve evidence safely, seek support and report the account or content.

WHAT TO DO IF IT HAPPENS TO YOU

Practical steps to protect yourself.

You do not need to know every technical detail to take the first steps. Start with what is safe and possible in your situation.

1

Do not blame yourself

Whether you trusted someone, shared a photograph or clicked a link, another person's decision to exploit you is not your fault.

2

Do not comply with threats

Avoid sending more images or paying money because someone threatens you. Compliance does not guarantee that the threats will stop.

3

Preserve evidence if safe

Keep relevant usernames, profile links, messages, payment demands, dates and screenshots. Do not download or forward sexual images of minors.

4

Secure your accounts

Change compromised passwords using a trusted device, enable two-factor authentication, review logged-in sessions and check recovery email addresses and phone numbers.

5

Use platform safety tools

Report abusive accounts or content, block or restrict the offender when safe, and review your privacy settings.

6

Tell someone you trust

Speak to a trusted adult, friend, guardian, counsellor or specialist support organisation. You should not have to manage threats alone.

7

Seek urgent help if you are in danger

If someone is threatening physical harm, stalking you offline or knows your location and may act on threats, prioritise your immediate safety and seek urgent assistance.

BUILDING DIGITAL SAFETY

Protect your accounts and personal information.

Digital safety is not about disappearing from the internet. It is about having more control over who can access you, your accounts and your information.

Use strong passwords

Use unique passwords for important accounts. A password manager can help you store them securely.

Enable two-factor authentication

Add another verification step to protect your accounts if someone discovers your password.

Review location sharing

Check whether apps, social media accounts or devices share your live location with other people.

Be careful with personal details

Think carefully before sharing your home address, school, workplace, daily routine or private contact details.

Check account access

Review connected devices, active sessions, third-party applications and account recovery options.

Pause before clicking

Be cautious with unexpected links, suspicious attachments, requests for verification codes and offers that seem too good to be true.

What if someone monitors your phone?

If you suspect that an abusive person has access to your device, changing settings or passwords may alert them. Consider using a trusted device to seek help and make a safety plan before making changes that could increase danger.

SUPPORT SOMEONE EXPERIENCING ONLINE ABUSE

Be part of their safety, not their shame.

People experiencing online exploitation may feel embarrassed, frightened or afraid of being blamed. Your response can make it easier for them to seek help.

Believe them

Listen without blaming them for trusting someone, sharing information or using the internet.

Protect their privacy

Never forward intimate content, screenshots or personal information to others as gossip or entertainment.

Help document the abuse

If safe and appropriate, help preserve messages, account details and other relevant evidence.

Help them access support

Assist them in reporting content, securing accounts and contacting a trusted person or appropriate service.

GET HELP & REPORT ONLINE ABUSE

You deserve support beyond the screen.

Online abuse can have offline consequences. If threats involve stalking, blackmail, sexual exploitation or physical danger, do not treat the situation as merely an internet disagreement.

Where to seek help

  • Platform reporting: Report abusive accounts, threats, impersonation and harmful content through the platform's official reporting tools.
  • Trusted adult or safeguarding contact: If you are under 18, speak to a safe adult who can help protect you and access appropriate support.
  • Cybercrime reporting: Where appropriate, seek assistance from relevant law enforcement or cybercrime reporting authorities.
  • Healthcare and counselling: Seek professional help if the experience is affecting your mental health, physical health or sense of safety.
  • Immediate danger: Contact local emergency services or a trusted person who can help you reach safety.
