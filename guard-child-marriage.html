HERGUARD / Forced & Child Marriage
HERGUARD · FREEDOM · SAFETY

Forced &
Child Marriage.

Marriage should never be a decision made through fear, threats, pressure or the absence of choice. Every girl deserves the opportunity to grow, learn, make decisions about her future and live with dignity.

If this is happening to you: You do not have to confront anyone or take a risky action to prove that you need help. Your safety matters. You deserve support, and there may be people and services able to help you consider your options.
01 · UNDERSTANDING

What is child or forced marriage?

These terms are related, but they describe different circumstances. Understanding the distinction helps us recognise the harm and the different ways people may need support.

Child marriage

A marriage or union in which at least one person is under 18. It can affect a child's safety, education, health, development, autonomy and future opportunities.

Forced marriage

A marriage entered into without the free and full consent of one or both people. Pressure may involve threats, coercion, emotional manipulation, financial control, violence or abuse of authority.

Can someone be forced to marry even if they don't say no?

Yes. Fear, family pressure, threats, dependence, intimidation or believing refusal is impossible can prevent someone from freely expressing their wishes. Silence or compliance under pressure should not automatically be treated as consent.

02 · RECOGNITION

Recognising the warning signs.

Forced marriage does not always begin with an announcement. Sometimes the warning signs appear gradually, and a girl may not know how to explain what she is experiencing.

Sudden marriage plans

A marriage being arranged unexpectedly, especially when a girl is not being given the opportunity to express her wishes.

Pressure to agree

Being told that she has no choice, that refusal is unacceptable, or that she must marry to protect the family's reputation.

Threats or punishment

Threats of violence, being sent away, losing financial support, being disowned, confinement or other consequences for refusing.

Withdrawal from school

Being removed from school, prevented from completing education or suddenly restricted from normal activities because of marriage plans.

Travel under pressure

Being pressured to travel or taken somewhere without being told the real purpose, particularly when marriage plans are involved.

Loss of freedom

Having communication, movement, friendships or access to trusted people restricted in preparation for a marriage she does not want.

03 · PRESSURE & COERCION

When choice is taken away.

Forced marriage may involve physical force, but it can also happen through emotional, financial, social or psychological pressure.

Emotional pressure

Being made to feel responsible for a family's happiness, reputation, shame, disappointment or wellbeing if she refuses.

Financial control

Threatening to withdraw necessities, school fees, housing, money or other support unless she agrees to marry.

Social or cultural pressure

Being told that family tradition, community expectations, religion or social standing make refusal impossible.

Threats and intimidation

Using threats, confinement, physical violence, intimidation or punishment to make someone accept a marriage.

Pressure is not consent.

A person's agreement should be freely given. Fear of punishment, abandonment, harm, humiliation or losing basic support can make it impossible to make a free decision.

04 · IMPACT

The impact can reach far beyond the wedding.

Child and forced marriage can affect different parts of a person's life. The experience is not the same for everyone, but the risks can be serious and long-lasting.

Education

Interrupted schooling, reduced access to further education, fewer opportunities and greater difficulty pursuing personal goals.

Physical health

Increased risks associated with early pregnancy, limited access to healthcare, reproductive health concerns and physical violence.

Mental wellbeing

Anxiety, isolation, distress, loss of autonomy, fear and difficulties coping with circumstances a person did not choose.

Freedom & independence

Reduced freedom to make decisions, maintain friendships, access resources, pursue interests or choose a future.

05 · IF YOU ARE AT RISK

You do not have to handle it alone.

If you believe a marriage is being arranged against your wishes, your circumstances and safety should guide what you do next. You do not need to wait until the situation becomes violent before seeking help.

Identify a safe person

Consider someone you trust who is likely to take you seriously: a teacher, counsellor, healthcare professional, trusted relative, safeguarding professional or support worker.

Explain what is happening

If it is safe, tell them clearly that you do not want the marriage, what you are being pressured to do, and whether you are afraid of what may happen if you refuse.

Think about immediate safety

If you may be harmed, confined or taken somewhere against your will, seek help from a safe person or appropriate protection service. Do not confront people or make plans that could put you at greater risk.

Consider access to important information

If safe and appropriate, know how to access important contact details, identification, medication and essential information. Do not attempt to gather or carry anything if doing so increases danger.

Seek specialised support

A child protection, gender-based violence, legal aid or safeguarding professional may help explain possible protections and plan next steps.

06 · SUPPORTING SOMEONE

If someone confides in you.

Someone facing forced marriage may feel trapped, guilty, frightened or conflicted about their family. Your response can help them feel less alone.

Take them seriously

Do not dismiss their fear or assume the family will eventually change its mind.

Listen without judgment

Avoid blaming them, questioning why they did not refuse sooner or forcing them to share more than they want.

Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep

Especially when a child may be at risk, explain gently that you may need to involve a safe professional who can help protect them.

Help them connect to support

Offer to help them contact a trusted professional or appropriate safeguarding service, without putting them at greater risk.

07 · GET HELP & REPORTING

Help should be reachable.

If you or someone you know is at risk of forced or child marriage, you deserve access to people who can listen, assess the situation and help you understand the available options.

Immediate danger

If someone is in immediate danger, seek help from a safe person nearby or an appropriate emergency or protection service. Avoid confronting the person creating the danger if that could increase the risk.

Child protection

If a child is at risk, a child protection professional, safeguarding officer or appropriate local authority may be able to assess the situation and help with protection.

Legal support

A qualified legal aid professional may explain applicable laws, protections, options and procedures in the person's location.

Trusted support

If formal services are difficult to reach, consider a safe adult, school safeguarding contact, healthcare professional or trusted organisation that takes protection seriously.

Report to MATONI

MATONI is working towards a safe, confidential and responsible support and reporting pathway. Our online reporting system is currently under development. This page does not submit a report, alert a responder or create a case. Please do not send sensitive identifying information through this page.

Important: This page is for educational purposes and does not replace professional safeguarding, emergency, legal or clinical support. Laws, reporting obligations and protection options differ by location and age. MATONI does not currently operate a staffed emergency response or reporting service.
